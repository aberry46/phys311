What surprised me most about designing for a user rather than myself was the shift from needing to complete a product, to needing to polish one. Having to give attention to presentation and accessibility is extremely important when factoring in that other people will be using this tool as well. This week, I had to consider whether someone unfamiliar with my code could actually understand and interact with it. Adding controls, visualizations, and direct manipulation made me realize that perhaps a technically accurate simulation is not necessarily an effective one should the user not be able to interpret what is happening. 

For my final project, I am considering re-creating "Angry Birds" but with the addition of way too much physics. I want it to be incredibly realistic, almost laughably so. I believe it will be both fun and instructive to those using it. Ideally, I want the final product to be something that is technically meaningful but also something that is interesting enough to explore.
